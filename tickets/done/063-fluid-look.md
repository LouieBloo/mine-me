# 063 - Fluid look: smooth surface, waves, depth shading, streams

**Priority:** P2 - **Status:** Done (2026-10-13); judged in the game - **Depends on:** 051, 060

## Problem
Fluids are drawn as one flat rectangle per cell, with a 3px lighter strip on top of each surface cell. Neighbouring cells at different levels make hard steps with a flat line at each one, and fluid falling through open air is a full-width block. A mine game is blocky, but the fluid should not be.

## Decisions (063 Q&A 2026-10-13)
- **Per-fluid data (owner):** the look is data, not constants. One nullable `Fluid.appearance` Json (shared `FluidAppearance`: `waveAmplitude`, `waveSpeed`, `highlight`, `depthDarkening`, `depthOpacity`), missing fields default. Edited on the admin Fluids page with the live preview showing it.
- **Surface (owner: "1, 3 and 2 together"):** surface cells in the same row blend their heights at the shared edge, so a settling puddle is a continuous slope, not steps. A real cliff (different rows) stays a vertical edge.
- **Waves (owner):** only the animated surface edge is redrawn per frame; the fluid body stays in the dirty-chunk graphics. Waves taper to nothing at walls and cliffs so they never open a gap.
- **Depth (owner):** fluid gets darker and more opaque the more fluid is above it, strength per fluid; the hard 3px strip becomes a soft highlight under a thin bright line.
- **Falling fluid (owner):** drawn as a narrower stream, not a full-width block ("it shouldn't be all blocks"). A cell is a stream when fluid has air (not wall or fluid) on both sides; a one-wide shaft with walls on both sides stays full. Width follows the level.
- Geometry is pure and in `packages/shared` so the game (Pixi) and the admin preview (canvas) draw the same shapes. `layoutFluidCell` stays: fire placement uses it.

## Implementation notes
- **Schema/shared:** `Fluid.appearance Json?` (additive, `db push` applied); `FluidAppearance`, `DEFAULT_FLUID_APPEARANCE`, `FLUID_APPEARANCE_LIMITS`, `resolveFluidAppearance`, `validateFluidAppearance` (part of `validateFluid`); `fluidShape.ts` (`layoutFluidShape`, `fluidBodyBands`, `fluidSurfaceWave`, `fluidWaveHeight`, `fluidDepthShade`, `bandBelowLine`) and `fluidColor.ts` (colour helpers moved here from the client).
- **Server:** the admin PUT/POST take and validate `appearance`; the client definition carries it (`appearance` is always filled in).
- **Client:** `FluidRenderer` draws shaded body slices per dirty chunk, plus a surface layer per chunk redrawn each frame only if some surface waves (`update(timeSec)` from the ticker). `setSolidSampler` (from the tile grid) enables streams. A changed cell also dirties its side neighbours, the chunk above, and the fluid stacked below it (shading depth).
- **Admin:** five look inputs under Appearance on the fluid page; the live preview draws the same shapes on a canvas, redrawn every 33 ms so waves move while the simulation still steps at the game's rate.
- **Tests:** shared shape/colour/data (35), server endpoint (3) and wire (1), client renderer (8 new), admin preview (4 new) and page (3 new). Mutation-checked: the shared-edge blend, the wave pin at walls, the per-frame redraw rule and the neighbour-chunk dirtying each fail a test.
- Not covered automatically: the Pixi drawing itself and the scene hookup; judge in the game. Existing fluids use the defaults until tuned on the Fluids page (thick oil wants low, slow waves).
- Also fixed: `MiningGameEngine.fluidWire.test.ts` no longer expects oil to have no flame (the dev seed gives it a light). `add-oil-fluid.test.ts` still fails on its own: it expects oil to generate from depth 8, but the dev mirror now says 2.

## Follow-up: flicker while fluid pours over a cell (2026-10-13)
- **Cause (found by tracing the real simulation):** the cell below a thin, flickering film of fluid held a steady, rising level, but the old rule "any fluid above means draw full" snapped it between ~40% and 100% every time the film appeared and vanished. Thin films beside a falling cell also flipped it between "stream" and "puddle".
- **Fix (shared `fluidShape.ts`):** the fill now ramps with how much is above (`FLUID_COVER_LEVEL`) instead of switching; a thin drip falling onto fluid that is not nearly full is not drawn (`FLUID_DRIP_LEVEL`); thin side films no longer decide what is a stream; depth counts partial cells. Same pour measured before/after: oil 122 -> 5 flicker events, water 92 -> 41 (the rest is the simulation's own two-step ping-pong at a high flow rate).
- **Tests:** ramp is continuous, a film barely moves a pool, drips hidden but a drip on a full cell and a floor puddle are drawn. Mutation-checked (old binary rule fails 3 tests, no drip rule fails 1).

## Follow-up 2: flicker at any level, and two surface lines stacked (2026-10-13)
- **Diagnosis (harness over three real pours: tank with shelf and gate, staircase of ledges, two basins):** the simulation itself ping-pongs rarely for oil (0-9 events). Most flicker was the drawing: cells whose level held steady were drawn alternately ~43 and ~55px tall, because per-cell yes/no rules read neighbours (is the cell above a stream, is this cell covered, is the cell beside a pool) and the simulation alternates its spreading direction every step, so those answers flipped every step. Stacked surface lines: the "cover" ramp only reached full at 0.5, so a full cell under a thinner one still counted as a surface (237-444 cases per pour).
- **Fix 1, geometry (shared `FluidLayout`, replaces `layoutFluidShape`):** every rule is a ramp. Each column's fluid is a body packed onto its floor, so a body has exactly one surface. Fluid rests on the cell below in proportion to how full that cell is; the falling rest joins the body if fluid is beside it and is a stream if not, in proportion to how much is beside it. Neighbouring surfaces join by how close they are (fully when level, not at all a tile apart or with terrain between). Stream width goes to nothing with its fluid, so streams never pop in or out.
- **Fix 2, easing (shared `FluidDisplay`):** the client and the admin preview draw an eased copy of the levels (0.12 s), so the fluid flows between the 10 Hz steps and the simulation's step-to-step pulsing is damped to under 40%. Bodies, sounds and fire still read the exact levels. Snapshots and fluid coming into view show at once; tick changes ease (`FluidRenderer.apply(cells, smooth)`, per-frame `update`).
- **Result, same pours:** stacked surfaces 237-444 -> 0; draw flicker (6px swings) 5-76 -> 0. Only 3-7px wobbles remain, in falling streams.
- **Tests:** `fluidPourRegression.test.ts` runs the three pours frame by frame and requires zero stacked surfaces and zero flicker (fails 3/3 with easing off, 3/3 with a surface on every cell); layout (one surface per body whatever the levels, packing, smooth growth, joins and their limits, streams thinning to nothing and joining smoothly), `FluidDisplay` (8), renderer easing (5). Mutation-checked: support chain, join distance, side joining as on/off.
- **Gaps closed (2026-10-13):** flames now rise from the drawn surface (`FluidLayout.surfaceAt` via `FluidRenderer.drawnSurfaceY`: the body a cell's fluid is packed into, eased), so they follow the fluid you see; the old per-cell `layoutFluidCell` is deleted (nothing used it and it encoded the old rule). Mining, placing or blowing up a tile redraws the fluid around it (`FluidRenderer.onTileChanged`, from every revealed tile in a tick), since terrain decides whether fluid rests, falls or joins. One cached layout per change serves drawing and fire. `add-oil-fluid.test.ts` no longer asserts tuned values from the dev mirror (it follows admin edits); it checks Oil generates with valid settings.

## Fixes (2026-10-16, owner report with screenshot)
- **Falling water not drawn in a one-wide shaft (e.g. over a ladder):** falling fluid between two walls was packed onto the bottom of its column, so the water in the cells above was drawn nowhere. Falling fluid in a shaft is now a centred stream (it still packs into the body once the cells under it are full). Tests in `fluidShape.test.ts`; mutation-checked.
- **Jagged surface:** neighbouring surfaces only joined when they were less than a tile apart, leaving a vertical riser at every cell edge on a heap. They now join whenever the two bodies overlap vertically (fluid beside fluid), so a heap slopes smoothly; a wall, a ledge edge or a different fluid still keeps them apart. The wall test now looks at the whole shared stretch of rows instead of one row picked from the surface heights, which flipped as the surface crossed a cell boundary (caught by the pour regression test).

## Flowing between cells (2026-10-16, second owner report: dashed falls, vertical edge at a lip)
- **Assessment:** everything was decided per cell from that cell's own level. A steady fall is only ~0.06 of a cell per cell, so each cell drew a short bar that did not reach up to the cell above (the dashes). The cell at a lip belonged to the same column as the shaft below, so its water was packed onto the floor far below and nothing was drawn there (the vertical edge).
- **Owner decisions:** "flow paths" (keep the renderer, change the layout), and a visible trickle (a fall is at least 1/8 of a cell wide).
- **Layout (shared `FluidLayout`):** each cell's fluid is resting, packed into the pool it is part of, falling (air on both sides), or **spilling** (fluid beside it, a drop under it). Falls are laid out per column as one segment from start to landing (one width from the whole run's flow, one x, pressed to the side the water pours over from), bridging the one-cell beats the simulation hands a fall down in and the gap right above the pool it lands in. The lip cell draws a **spill**: a sheet from the pool's surface at the edge, arcing out over the lip and down into the jet, with the surface highlight carried along its edge (`spillEdge`). Every switch is a ramp (spill vs blob, which side, the arc), checked by the pour regression scenes.
- **Also fixed:** body strips clamped a steep surface at each end instead of following it, cutting pale wedges (and leaving hairline slivers) out of sloped surfaces; crossings now land exactly on the strip edge and a strip's outline stops where the surface meets its bottom. Wave fills under half a pixel are skipped.
- **Client:** redraws follow a fall across one-cell gaps; the spill edge is stroked in the game and the admin preview.
- **Tests:** `fluidFalls.test.ts` (the owner's scene through the real sim and easing: an unbroken jet of one width and place, the jet under the lip pressed to the pool's side, the spill joining the pool surface to the jet, beats bridged), strip-geometry tests (follows the line, no sliver), pour regressions (no flicker), renderer tests. Mutation-checked: bridging, spills, strip crossings, redraw across gaps, the spill highlight.

## Simulation fixes (2026-10-16, owner report: one sandbox click shows three thin columns far apart)
The drawing was not the root cause; the simulation was. Found by stepping one poured cell through the real grid:
- **Falling was scaled by the flow rate.** Only 60% of a falling full cell of water dropped each step (6% for lava); the rest spread sideways in mid-air, so one click became a 0.60 cell under a row of three thin cells (0.08 / 0.20 / 0.12) that fell forever as three separate thin jets. Falling into room below is now gravity (everything that fits drops at once); the flow rate only paces spreading and pressure.
- **A steady stream fell asleep.** Only cells whose level changed stayed awake; a cell in a steady stream passes on exactly what it receives, so it slept mid-stream and the column jammed into 1.00 / 0.00 packets. Cells that fluid flowed through stay awake.
- **The fall used the cell below's level from before it moved.** Rows go bottom first, so the cell below has already emptied this step; a falling column now falls into the room that is really there.
- **Fluid pouring off an edge only moved at the levelling rate** (half the difference x flow rate), so landing fluid piled into a heap faster than it could run off. Towards a neighbour with room under it, all of the difference moves at the flow rate (a ramp on how much room).
- **Sandbox pour** tops the tile up to one full cell instead of adding a cell every 60 ms on top of what is there (an overfull source cell was pushed sideways in mid-air).
- **Drawing:** a stream is as wide as the water it carries (a full cell falling fills the tile; it was 1.5x the level but capped at 65%), the width is measured over the cells that are really falling (a mostly-resting landing cell no longer drags it around), a short fall in mid-air is drawn as one compact blob at its centre of weight, and every surface runs through each column's own height (left edge, centre, right edge) so a column standing higher than its neighbours is no longer cut down by the joins.
- **Tests:** `fluidFalling.test.ts` (a full cell of water or lava falls whole, steady and heavy pours stay one even column, it spreads only once landed), blob size and smooth hand-over, pour top-up. Mutation-checked: the fall rate, the delta-aware fall, the awake rule, the pour cap.
- **Known limit, not fixed here:** the client only receives levels, so it cannot tell a column of full cells falling at full speed from one standing still, and its easing smears a falling blob over a few cells (indistinguishable from a short stream by level alone). See the follow-up decision.

## The server says what falls (2026-10-16, owner decision: "server says what falls", keep the current speed)
- **Why:** with levels alone the client cannot tell a column of full cells falling at full speed from one standing still, and its easing smeared a falling blob over several cells (indistinguishable from a short stream).
- **Simulation:** `FluidGrid` records how much fell into each cell from the cell above in the last step (`getFalling`); a cell whose falling amount changes is reported even when its level does not (a stream stopping).
- **Wire:** `FluidWireCell` gains an optional fifth value, the quantised falling amount (left off when nothing falls); the join snapshot and revealed tiles (`fluid: [id, level, falling?]`) carry it too, so a client that first sees a stream mid-flow knows it is falling.
- **Client:** `FluidField`/`FluidDisplay` keep it. Fluid in free fall (`freeFallShare`: its falling share, times nothing resting under it to hold it up) is drawn sliding down from the cell above over the step (`FluidLayout` `fallProgress`; the renderer redraws it every frame until it arrives). The cell it fell into and the cell it left ease almost at once (`FluidDisplay.hurry`, scaled by `fallHurry`: free fall times how much the fall emptied the cell above), so a blob is not smeared; a steady stream keeps the slow easing that damps the simulation's pulsing. Falling fluid beside fluid it is joining, or onto a heap or pool, is drawn where it is (the server's value pulses there).
- **Also:** spills are taken from the pool beside the lip (its surface and floor), not from the row the fall starts in, and only count where that pool sits on ground (the side of a heap is not a lip); falling fluid below a lip does not fill the drop; sideways spreading works out both sides from the same amount (it pulsed); a fall no longer bridges a gap above a pool when something is still falling under the gap (two falls overlapped). The admin preview draws the same way.
- **Tests:** `fluidFallingWire.test.ts` (falling recorded and reported on its own, wire value, hurry, a blob sliding at constant size), renderer slide redraws, server wire (ticks, snapshot, revealed). The pour regressions now measure what is drawn inside each cell (every polygon clipped to the cell) instead of crediting a shape to the cell that owns it. Mutation-checked: the falling-only report, the slide, the hurry, the per-frame redraw.
- **Known, by decision:** holding the sandbox pour adds a full cell every step, more than the grid moves sideways at 10 steps a second, so a mound builds under it (the fall itself now draws as a full-width jet). Revisit with the speed question if it bothers.
